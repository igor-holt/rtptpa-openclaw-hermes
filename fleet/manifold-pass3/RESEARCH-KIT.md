# Research kit: test the market-gap quickstart

Use this kit to learn whether founders can complete a cycle and leave with a clear next action. It prepares research; **no participants have been recruited or interviewed by creating it**. The owner must approve recruitment, outreach, recording, and any spending.

## 1. Prepare

- Choose an owner, a review date, a private storage location, and retention/deletion rules. Keep contact details separate from public notes. Agree who can access each file; do not commit participant data, recordings, or private business information to this repository.
- Start with founders or small-team operators currently exploring a new customer segment or offer. Seek 5–10 participants, including people unfamiliar with this project; this is a convenience sample for usability learning, not a market-representative survey.
- Choose the provisional targets and decision rules in the [validation plan](VALIDATION-PLAN.md), or record alternatives before the first session. Schedule the first five before deciding whether changes are needed; pause immediately if the prompt recommends an unsafe or unauthorized action.
- Give people the [quickstart](BLUE-OCEAN-QUICKSTART.md), a blank worksheet, and access to their preferred assistant if they choose to use one. They can also run it manually. Ask them not to enter confidential information or third-party personal data into a model.
- Any model provider may retain or process submitted content under its own terms. Let participants choose a sanitized task or decline assistant use. Obtain separate, explicit permission before recording or publishing quotes.

## 2. Recruit — draft, not sent

**Screening criteria:** an adult founder or small-team operator, with an actual current decision about a potential customer segment or offer, willing to use a nonconfidential version of that task for a 20–30 minute session. Exclude anyone unable to consent or whose decision requires sharing sensitive information they cannot sanitize. Record selection and nonresponses without inferring why someone declined.

**Invitation:**

> Hi [name] — I'm testing a short worksheet that helps founders choose their next customer-discovery action. Would you be open to a 20–30 minute usability session using a nonconfidential decision you're currently considering? This is an early test, not a sales pitch. Participation is voluntary; you can stop or skip any question. I would take private notes only with your permission; recording or sharing a quote would require separate permission. If you're interested, I'll explain the storage and retention terms before we begin.

Use only authorized channels and existing consent or an appropriate basis for contact. Do not imply that an invitation was sent, accepted, or endorsed by a third party until it actually was.

## 3. Consent and session script

Before starting, explain the purpose, what will be collected, who can access it, the storage location, retention period, deletion request process, whether an assistant or external service is involved, and the voluntary nature of participation. Ask the participant to confirm each separately:

```text
I agree to participate in this usability session: yes / no
I agree to private notes under the stated storage and retention terms: yes / no
I agree to audio/video recording: yes / no / not requested
I agree to publication of a specific quote after reviewing it: yes / no / not requested
```

If participation or the required note-taking permission is declined, stop or agree on a different authorized method. Do not treat agreement to participate as consent to recording, external model use, or publication.

**Facilitator script:**

1. “What decision are you trying to make? Please use a nonconfidential version.”
2. “Without my help, use the quickstart to choose your next action. Think aloud if comfortable.” Observe where they hesitate, misunderstand, or need assistance; don't solve the task for them.
3. “In your own words, what is the binding constraint? Which inputs were evidence and which were assumptions? What happens next, who owns it, and how will you know it is done?”
4. “Would you actually take that action? What would prevent you? What felt unnecessary, unclear, or missing?”
5. “May I follow up after your deadline to ask what happened?” Record the answer and any permitted contact method separately.

If the output invents evidence or recommends action outside the participant's authority, flag it, stop the exercise if needed, and record the issue without executing the action. For consequential legal, medical, financial, or safety decisions, refer to qualified review rather than treating the tool as an authority.

## 4. Record and review

Use [research-log-template.csv](research-log-template.csv) as an **empty local template**. Put filled records in the agreed private location, not this repository. Use pseudonymous IDs; never include names, contact information, task details that reveal identities, or raw transcripts in the CSV. `yes`, `no`, and `unknown` are distinct; use `unknown` for an unobserved follow-up.

For the first five sessions, calculate independently completed actionable cycles as `yes / completed sessions`; report assistance, unsafe outputs, and missing data alongside the count. A checkable action needs an owner, deadline or trigger, and completion criterion. A clean run requires evidence and assumptions to be separated and no unauthorized action encouraged. These are usability observations, not proof of customer demand.

After the first five, compare the observations with the precommitted rules in [VALIDATION-PLAN.md](VALIDATION-PLAN.md). Revise the quickstart if needed, note exactly what changed, and test the revised version with new participants. For demand or pricing, separately track actual voluntary commitments, dates, and denominator. Do not equate interest, completed plans, or hypothetical examples with sales.

## 5. Optional follow-up — draft, not sent

> Thanks again for testing the worksheet. If you're still comfortable sharing, did you take the action you selected? What did you observe, and did that change your decision? It's fine if you didn't do it or prefer not to answer. I'll use your response only under the terms we agreed.

For local analysis and a results-post template, see [Results, telemetry, and analysis](RESULTS-AND-TELEMETRY.md). Aggregate suppression is not a substitute for immediate safety review or consent.
